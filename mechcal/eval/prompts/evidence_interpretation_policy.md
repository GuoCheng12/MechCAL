Interpret the submitted argument, not its tone.

Read the finding, proposed mechanism link and limitations together before
selecting a rubric route. Distinguish these three situations:
- A proposed explanation is grounded in a submitted finding but its occurrence
  is unconfirmed. Assess the applicable partial routes; lack of confirmation
  alone neither removes the explanation nor proves a strong route.
- A finding is accurate but no mechanism-specific connection is articulated.
  Do not invent that connection for the submission. A list of numbers or a
  statement that a candidate deserves testing is not itself an explanation.
- A submitted observation contradicts the proposed link. Preserve that
  counterevidence; it is not merely cautious language or missing validation.

Near-equivalent phrasings must be assessed by the same criterion. In particular,
"this finding makes the pathway plausible, but does not establish it" and
"this finding makes the pathway plausible; its occurrence remains unresolved"
have the same evidential scope. Do not demand a keyword, affirmative grammar,
or removal of limitations to recognize a proposed connection. Conversely,
confident wording cannot supply a missing finding or mechanism link.
General scientific knowledge can explain the link actually proposed, without
turning a conditional assumption into an observed state, geometry or pathway.
If a transition is mentioned only to rule it out, do not recast it as support.

In the rationale identify the submitted mechanism link, or the specific missing
link, and explain whether the stated limitations bound that link or contradict
it. Evaluate factual errors independently of other valid support routes.
Do not penalize a report merely for disclosing uncertainty or method disagreement;
assess which particular interpretation the disagreement actually undermines.
Apply the same rules to structural reasoning and computational reports. Tool
access, verbosity, model identity and self-rated support are not scoring criteria.
No score is prescribed by these interpretation instructions. The unchanged
family-specific rubric determines whether a complete route exists.
